# Fail fast, resume later: taming Agmarknet pulls

**The data.gov.in feed cannot be made reliable from the client side, so the fix is to stop waiting on it and to make every pull small, resumable and re-tryable later in the day.** No official documentation, status page, outage notice or developer report about 503, 500 or stall behaviour for resource 9ef84268-... was found, so the root cause is unknown and the recommendations rest on standard resilience practice, not on anything the publisher has promised. Your current setup is the likeliest reason a bad day costs 6-7 minutes: each attempt may wait up to 120 s, and 4 retries at 5/10/20/40 s add about 75 s of sleeping that rarely helps against a gateway reporting no healthy backend. A switch of primary source is not justified: every official alternative found (Agmarknet 2.0, UPAg, state portals, e-NAM) is either a human-facing dashboard or sits upstream of the same data, and the one documented mirror (CEDA) is monthly and non-commercial. The practical plan is to (1) replace the hand-written loop with a layered timeout, retry and circuit-breaker pipeline using much shorter per-attempt limits, (2) track each pull as a checkpointed unit so a failure leaves a resumable remainder, (3) add a distinct "data not yet published" outcome for healthy empty responses, and (4) re-run on a schedule through the afternoon and evening instead of retrying in seconds. The prioritised change list is at the end. Throughout, "Fact" means a claim with a source and "Inference" means the researchers' own judgment.

## What is known, and not known, about the data.gov.in feed

**Sourced facts.** The resource is described by a vendor blog as the "Daily mandi price dataset" (2M+ records, updated daily), taking `limit`, `offset` and case-sensitive `filters[Field]` parameters; the same blog gives a 30 s client timeout and 0.3 s inter-request delay as its own example ([themineworks](https://themineworks.com/blog/india-government-data-api/)). Published limit guidance conflicts: that blog says "max 100 on most datasets" and no published rate limit, while another guide says a free tier of 1,000 requests per day ([India Data Atlas](https://indiadataatlas.deccanspatial.com/chapters/ch06-apis/data-gov-api/)). Neither is official, and your 250-row pages evidently work, so the 100 figure is not a hard cap for this resource. Other public projects show the pain indirectly: one uses `timeout=180` with no retry and falls back to a local CSV on any error or empty result ([HF Space](https://huggingface.co/spaces/RushiMane2003/market_trial/discussions/1/files)), and another ingests twice a day (or every 6 h) and shows a staleness banner after 30 h ([mandi-lens](https://github.com/soumyadip-admin/mandi-lens)). A third-party wrapper re-syncs at 8:30 PM IST, which is its own schedule but hints at evening publication ([mandi-api](https://mandi-api.vercel.app/blog/agmarknet-api-alternative)). The exact 503 text "No server is available to handle this request" is a generic load-balancer page; searches only surfaced it for an unrelated API ([Kite forum](https://kite.trade/forum/discussion/comment/47594/)), so attributing it to data.gov.in rests on your logs, not on any source.

**Researchers' inferences.** A 503 of that kind is server-side and transient, and not caused by your query shape. Retries seconds apart probably hit the same degraded backend, so spacing attempts over hours should work better than adding rapid ones. Since about 30 rows took 6-7 minutes, row count is not the bottleneck and shrinking pages is unlikely to help on its own. A slow first call followed by fast repeats (you saw a 17 s connect, then 0.1 s repeats) is consistent with a cold backend or connection queuing, but this is unconfirmed. Note also an inconsistency in your own log: "timed out (30s) on 5 attempts" does not match a 120 s HttpClient.Timeout, so check which timeout actually fired; it may be a different code path or a separate setting. Several other facts are unverified: the date-filter format (the response shows DD/MM/YYYY and one project sent `filters[Arrival_Date]` in that format before removing it, with no reason stated), whether filtered queries are faster, and publish times. Treat each as something to test against your own logs.

## Why your current timeouts and retries cost minutes

**Facts about the tooling.** `HttpClient.Timeout` is a single cancellation timer, and a known issue in dotnet/extensions showed it overriding a longer resilience-handler total timeout until it was set to `Timeout.InfiniteTimeSpan` ([dotnet/extensions#4770](https://github.com/dotnet/extensions/issues/4770)). `SocketsHttpHandler.ConnectTimeout` defaults to infinite and covers only the connection phase ([MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/api/system.net.http.socketshttphandler.connecttimeout)). The standard resilience handler stacks rate limiter, total timeout (30 s), retry (3 retries, 2 s exponential with jitter), circuit breaker (10% failure ratio, minimum throughput 100, 5 s break) and attempt timeout (10 s), and treats 5xx, 408, 429 and `HttpRequestException` as failures ([MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/core/resilience/http-resilience)). The default breaker will never open for a job making a few dozen requests, because it ignores the failure ratio until minimum throughput is reached ([Polly docs](https://www.pollydocs.org/strategies/circuit-breaker.html)). `HttpRetryStrategyOptions` supports `ShouldRetryAfterHeader` ([MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/api/microsoft.extensions.http.resilience.httpretrystrategyoptions)). Polly's own guidance is not to use retry for scheduling but to use a job scheduler for long-horizon repetition ([Polly docs](https://www.pollydocs.org/strategies/retry.html)).

**Inferences.** With a hand-written loop, the 120 s timeout most likely applies per attempt, so one dead page can burn up to 5 x 120 s plus about 75 s of backoff, roughly 11 minutes, before failing. That arithmetic matches your 6-7 minute runs and is the main thing to remove. The researchers suggest a starting point of ConnectTimeout 5-10 s, per-attempt 30-45 s (healthy 250-row pages should return in seconds), total per page about 100 s, 3 retries with jitter and a 30 s delay cap, and a breaker with a 60-120 s sampling window, minimum throughput 3-5 and a 30-60 s break, so one bad page trips it. Retry 408, 429, 502, 503, 504, network errors and timeouts, and allow only one retry of a 500, since 500s are often deterministic though here they may be overload. If a server `Retry-After` exceeds about 60 s, stop and defer instead of sleeping. Do not run both the hand-written loop and a resilience handler (4 x 4 attempts), and do not use hedging, which adds load on a fragile endpoint. Short per-attempt timeouts lose only if the server is slow but healthy; the adaptive remedy is a longer timeout on later attempts, and a run-level time budget (3-4 minutes) bounds everything. Smaller details such as IPv4 forcing, `PooledConnectionLifetime` of 2-5 minutes and an idle timeout near 1 minute are worth doing only if `curl -4` versus `curl -6` timings or stale-connection stalls show the need; the docs confirm DNS is resolved only at connection creation ([MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/fundamentals/networking/http/httpclient-guidelines)).

## Resumable, split and scheduled pulls beat longer waits

None of this section is source-backed beyond the Polly scheduling advice above; the researchers searched for engineering literature on resumable ingestion and found none, so it is design judgment. Treat each unit (a state or commodity slice for one business date, or a page of offsets) as independently checkpointed in a table such as `IngestionCheckpoint(Feed, BusinessDate, Partition, NextOffset, Status, Attempts, LastError, RowsFetched, UpdatedUtc)`. Upsert rows and advance the checkpoint in one transaction, with an idempotent upsert on market, commodity, variety, grade and arrival date, so overlapping or repeated pulls are harmless. If the API honours `filters[state]` (and the date filter, once the field casing is tested), splitting by state yields small requests that fail independently; otherwise keep offset paging, halve the page size after repeated timeouts and floor it at 25. After a unit exhausts its retries, mark it failed and continue; stop the whole run only when the circuit opens or the run budget elapses. A recurring tick every 30-60 minutes inside a window (for example 10:00-20:00 IST, plus a late catch-up), implemented as a `BackgroundService` with `PeriodicTimer`, Quartz.NET or Hangfire, asks whether the day's feed is complete and exits immediately if so. Add jitter, a lock so slow runs never overlap, and move the first attempt toward the hours your own logs show are healthiest. Given the evening-sync hint above, expect the feed for a day to be complete late, not early.

## A "not yet published" outcome and logging make failures legible

**Inference-based design.** Many of your 42 pulls succeeded with 0 rows because the feed was empty for that date. Those should not look like successes or failures. Return a run outcome of `Complete`, `Partial`, `NotPublished`, `Unavailable` or `Failed`, where `NotPublished` means the server answered HTTP 200 with zero records for the business date. It triggers no retry storm, only a later tick, and the app should keep serving yesterday's data stamped "as of" with a staleness banner, mirroring the 30 h banner pattern in mandi-lens. Guard against treating a 200 with an HTML body or a schema change as empty: the wrapper blog warns that response schemas vary across resources and over time, so validate the JSON shape and treat HTML as a transient 5xx. Avoid any silent fallback to fake data, which an open-source Agmarknet MCP server does when its key is invalid ([Glama](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/Krishna-Baldwa/agmarket-mcp/schema)).

**Logging facts and design.** Polly emits events for circuit transitions, retries (`OnRetry`, not on the first call) and timeouts (`OnTimeout`, the reliable signal when an outer retry swallows `TimeoutRejectedException`) ([Polly timeout](https://www.pollydocs.org/strategies/timeout.html)). Log one structured line per attempt with run id, unit or offset, attempt number, status code or exception type (including inner type), which timeout fired, elapsed milliseconds, rows received and Retry-After, plus one summary per run with outcome, counts and next try time; persist the last N summaries so the UI can say "10:30 server unavailable, 3 units missing, next try 11:00". The names of .NET 10 HTTP metrics were not verified.

## Alternatives: keep data.gov.in primary, hedge cheaply

Facts: the data.gov.in dataset is generated through AGMARKNET ([data.gov.in](https://data.gov.in/resource/current-daily-price-various-commodities-various-markets-mandi), seen only as a search snippet; fetch returned 403, and the snippet's "API does not exist" wording is unreconciled with your working key). Agmarknet 2.0 launched in November 2025 with 4,367 mandis linked and no mention of downloads or an API ([AgriTimes](https://agritimes.co.in/agmarknet-2-0-e-nam-boost-real-time-mandi-price-transparency)); UPAg has no documented public API ([Vikaspedia](https://en.vikaspedia.in/viewcontent/agriculture/agri-directory/unified-portal-for-agricultural-statistics?lgn=en)); no official e-NAM price API was found; state systems such as UP e-Mandi push into Agmarknet 2.0 ([Hans India](https://www.thehansindia.com/news/national/yogi-government-connects-all-251-up-mandis-to-national-network-1127009)). CEDA (Ashoka University) offers an API with 300+ commodities and 2,700+ mandis, but updates monthly and permits non-commercial use only with mandatory credit ([CEDA terms](https://ceda.ashoka.edu.in/api-terms-conditions/)). RapidAPI and Apify wrappers pull from data.gov.in itself ([DEV](https://dev.to/shariqmirza/i-built-a-real-time-indian-mandi-price-api-heres-how-50k9)). GODL-India generally permits commercial reuse with attribution, though only secondary sources were read, not the gazette ([Wikimedia](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Template:GODL-India-text)). No Agmarknet terms or robots.txt were found, and its report forms appear to use captchas.

Inferences: the data.gov.in API is the cleanest legal path; scraping Agmarknet 2.0 is unverified and must not bypass captchas. A 503 may be a gateway problem and not an Agmarknet outage, but a direct route would only exist via scraping. Ranked fallbacks: your own stored last-good data with retries; a second data.gov.in listing ("variety wise daily market prices", details unverified) or a downloadable file if the portal offers one; a written request through the portal's "Request API" channel or to Agmarknet/NIC for a more reliable feed or higher quota; CEDA only for historical backfill and only after a commercial licence is cleared; MSAMB and similar state portals for manual spot checks. Open questions: whether bulk CSV download exists for this resource, whether any 503s are quota-related, and the actual publish time.

## Conclusion

The sharpest finding is that the reported failures are mostly self-inflicted in their duration: the endpoint's flakiness is not solvable client-side, but the 6-7 minute runs are, because per-attempt waits of up to 120 s plus tight-interval retries convert a fast-detectable outage into a long one. Equally important, an empty response and a failed response are different events, and your history of many zero-row successes suggests that a large share of "problems" is the feed not yet being published, which needs a patient scheduler and not a retry loop.

Evidence quality is the main caveat: apart from Microsoft and Polly documentation, almost everything about the API itself is third-party or absent, and the job-design guidance is judgment. The cheapest way to firm up the plan is to instrument first (change 8 with the timeouts of changes 1-2), collect a week of failure-versus-hour and publish-time data, and then set the schedule, page size and splitting strategy from that evidence instead of from the starting values here.
